# Canadian Baseline Cyber Security Controls Mapping: IT@serve Company

*Fictional Canadian organization. Part 3 of 3. Version 0.3 (refreshed after the Canadian revision of Parts 1 and 2), November 2026.*

The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security publishes *Baseline Cyber Security Controls for Small and Medium Organizations* (version 1.2). It has 13 baseline controls (BC.1 to BC.13) that apply an 80/20 idea: most of the benefit for a fifth of the effort. Clinics and insurers in Canada recognize it, so IT@serve should be able to show how it meets each control.

**How it fits IT@serve.** The baseline says it is intended for organizations whose potential injury from a breach is at or below "medium", and it points organizations with higher risk to ISO/IEC 27001, NIST CSF or similar. IT@serve handles patient health information and has privileged access to many clinics, so it works to ISO 27001 and CSF and treats the baseline as a **minimum** that its clinics will expect. Where the baseline lists a control, IT@serve's actions usually go further, shown in the last column.

> **Caveat:** the mapping to Annex A and CSF 2.0 is my own analysis, and the summaries of the controls are in my own words. Read the original document for exact wording. Source: https://cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/baseline-cyber-security-controls-small-and-medium-organizations

**Reading the status:** "Partly in place" means at least one mapped Annex A control is already implemented or partially implemented in the Statement of Applicability. "Planned" means the control is not yet in place and an action is planned. 5 of 13 baseline controls are partly in place and 8 are planned.

The baseline also lists organizational controls (OC.1 to OC.5), such as naming someone responsible for IT security and committing to progressive improvement. IT@serve meets these through its ISMS scope (Part 1), the Security and Compliance Lead role and this improvement roadmap.
